A residual archive of AI application memory

Gone from
the source. Still in
the machine.

When an AI app reads a document, it rarely keeps just one copy. It cuts search chunks, writes summaries and saves past answers. Delete the original, and those copies can stay behind: still retrievable, still quotable.

GhostCache plants a fictional secret in one document, deletes only that source, catches the model repeating the secret anyway, then follows provenance to clear every managed copy, and checks that the facts that should survive still do.

The experiment, in three plates

What a deletion leaves behind.

  1. Plate I

    The source disappears.

    A fictional passphrase lives in one document, d-vault. The experiment deletes that document, and only that document: its body is emptied and its title replaced.

  2. Plate II

    The copies remain.

    Its search chunk, three summaries and a saved answer stay active. Ask in different words, and retrieval hands those copies to the model, which repeats the secret.

  3. Plate III

    Follow the trail.

    Every derived artifact records what it was made from. Cleanup walks that provenance, empties every descendant, rebuilds a summary from the surviving documents, then checks that the secret is gone and legitimate facts still answer.

Scope

What this shows, precisely.

The source-only deletion is a deliberately broken control built into GhostCache, not a finding about any other product. Dependency tracking and cache invalidation are established techniques; GhostCache makes the failure and its cleanup visible.

Read the method and its limits →

Covered

  • GhostCache's own managed memory: artifact titles and bodies in SQLite
  • The provenance it recorded for every derived artifact
  • Its retrieval and answer paths, checked with four defined questions
  • Facts that must survive: an unrelated time and an independently sourced name

Not covered

  • Model weights: there is no unlearning claim
  • Secure erasure of disk pages, journals or backups
  • Saved reports, screenshots and this site, which keep the fictional marker as evidence
  • Logs, browser history and other RAG systems
